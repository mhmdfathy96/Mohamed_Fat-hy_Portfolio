# Upwork Video Introduction — Scene & Script

**Goal:** let a client hear you explain your own work, so they judge you on delivery
rather than on a profile label. Target **50–60 seconds**. Check Upwork's current max
length before you record — it has changed before.

---

## 1. The setup

### Camera
- **Phone, horizontal (landscape).** Rear camera, not the selfie camera — it's sharper.
- Lens at **eye level**. Stack it on books if you have to. Shooting up your nose reads
  amateur; shooting down reads timid.
- **Distance:** arm's length plus a little. Frame from mid-chest to just above your head.
- Leave a little space above your head, not a lot. Your eyes should sit roughly one-third
  down the frame.
- **Lock focus and exposure** before you roll (tap and hold on your face).

### Light
- **Face a window.** Daylight, no direct sun. This one choice does more than any gear.
- Never sit with the window *behind* you — you'll be a silhouette.
- Shoot mid-morning or late afternoon. Midday sun is harsh, evening lamps go orange.

### Sound
Audio matters more than picture here, and it matters much more than usual for you —
see the delivery notes below.

- **Use wired earphones with a mic**, or a cheap lav clipped under your chin. Do not
  rely on the phone's built-in mic from arm's length.
- Small room with soft things in it (bed, curtains, sofa). Bare walls echo.
- Kill the fan and the AC for 60 seconds. Phone on silent, not vibrate.

### Background
- Simple and tidy. A plain wall, a bookshelf, or a desk with one or two things on it.
- **No** virtual backgrounds or blur filters. They look like a video call, not a portfolio.
- Nothing distracting behind your head (a plant that looks like it grows out of your skull).

### Wardrobe
- Solid colour, mid-tone. A plain shirt or a good t-shirt.
- Avoid pure white (blows out), pure black (crushes), and fine stripes/checks (they shimmer).

## 2. The script (~55 seconds)

Numbers are written out the way you should *say* them.

> **[0:00 – 0:07] — Hook**
>
> "Hi, I'm Mohamed. I build AI into products that people actually use."

> **[0:07 – 0:21] — One concrete result**
>
> "Last year I built a support assistant trained on a client's own documents.
> It now answers about eighty percent of their support tickets — with citations,
> so nobody has to guess where the answer came from."

> **[0:21 – 0:36] — Why you're different**
>
> "Most AI projects stop at the demo. Mine don't. I also build the backend and the
> mobile app around them. Spring Boot on the server. Flutter on the phone.
> One person, the whole stack."

> **[0:36 – 0:48] — Credibility**
>
> "Sixteen projects on Upwork, and a hundred percent job success score.
> My apps are live on the App Store, Google Play, and Huawei AppGallery —
> one of them past fifty thousand downloads."

> **[0:48 – 0:58] — Close**
>
> "I'm based in Cairo, and I work with clients across Europe, the Gulf, and the US.
> If you have an AI idea and you want it to actually ship — send me a message."

## 3. Delivery notes

Your profile lists English as **Conversational**, so the video is doing real work here:
it lets a client hear that you communicate clearly, instead of reading a label and
guessing. The script is built for that on purpose.

- **Every sentence is short.** That's deliberate. Short sentences survive an accent;
  long ones don't. Don't stitch them together.
- **Slower than feels natural.** When you're nervous you speed up. Aim for about
  20% slower than your instinct.
- **Full stop = real pause.** Breathe at each one. The pauses read as confidence.
- **Accent is fine. Mumbling is not.** Nobody is grading your accent — they are checking
  whether they can follow you. Open your mouth more than feels normal.
- Rehearse out loud **five or six times** before recording. Not to memorise it word for
  word — to stop being surprised by your own sentences.
- Smile on the first line and the last line. Neutral in the middle is fine.

### Practical

- Record it in **one take, several times.** Ten mediocre takes beat one over-edited take.
- Between takes, don't stop the camera — pause, breathe, start the line again.
- If you fluff a word, **keep going**. You'll almost always fix it in a later take.
- Look at the **lens**, not at yourself on the screen. Put a sticker or an arrow next to
  the lens if it helps.

---

## 4. Do not

- ❌ Read off a script on screen. The eye movement is obvious and it kills trust instantly.
- ❌ List your whole tech stack. That's what your profile is for.
- ❌ Say "I am passionate about coding" or "I deliver high quality work on time."
  Every other video says this. Yours says *eighty percent of support tickets*.
- ❌ Open with "My name is Mohamed and I am a software engineer with X years of experience."
  Dead on arrival. Lead with what you build.
- ❌ Add music, captions, logos, or transitions. Just you, talking.

---

## 5. Before you upload — checklist

- [ ] Landscape, not portrait
- [ ] Face lit from the front, no window behind you
- [ ] Audio clean — listen back **on headphones** before you commit
- [ ] Under Upwork's max length
- [ ] You looked at the lens, not the screen
- [ ] The eighty-percent number is stated clearly — it's the strongest thing you say
- [ ] Nothing in the background you'd be embarrassed by
